Every few years, a shiny new note-taking startup promises to organize your entire brain forever. Five years later, that startup pivots to enterprise workflows, locks basic features behind a subscription tier, alters its proprietary schema, or quietly shuts down its servers—leaving behind an awkward export button that produces broken HTML or unreadable JSON.

Apps are temporary. Your thinking should not be.

That tension sits at the heart of **"File over app"**, a digital philosophy popularized by [Steph Ango](https://stephango.com/file-over-app), CEO of Obsidian. Its premise is deceptively simple: ==if you want your writing to outlive the software you compose it in, the files must come first==.

> If you want to be able to read your writing on a computer in the 2060s or 2160s, it helps if your files are simple enough to have been read on a computer in the 1960s.

Plain text is the closest thing computing has to archival cotton paper.

## Why Proprietary Vaults Fail the 20-Year Test

When notes are trapped inside a remote database or an opaque binary blob, your access depends on a long chain of fragile assumptions:

1. **Vendor Solvency**: The company hosting your notes must remain profitable and operational indefinitely.
2. **Format Stability**: Future versions of the application must maintain backward compatibility with older schemas.
3. **Account Access**: You must retain access to the email address, two-factor device, or subscription billing required to unlock your own words.
4. **Export Integrity**: When you finally decide to migrate away, the exporter must not strip headings, checkboxes, or nested lists.

Even well-intentioned cloud apps routinely stumble on step four. Block-based editors serialize paragraphs into deeply nested UUID trees that flatten poorly into standard text, while proprietary rich-text formats require custom parsers just to extract a bulleted list.

## The Anatomy of a Portable Markdown Note

Markdown succeeds where proprietary formats fail because it is legible both as **raw plain text** and as **structured markup**. Open a `.md` file in Apple TextEdit, VS Code, Vim, Obsidian, Bear, iA Writer, or a terminal `cat` command forty years from now, and every heading, emphasis mark, and checklist remains immediately clear to human eyes.

When Fold serializes a note for export, it avoids custom JSON wrappers around prose and outputs clean CommonMark / GitHub Flavored Markdown paired with standard YAML frontmatter:

```markdown title="studio-craft-notes.md"
---
title: "Hardware-grade craft notes"
date: 2026-11-05
folder: "Ideas"
tag: "craft"
starred: true
pinned: false
---

# Hardware-grade craft notes

Fold is built around **concentric double-framing**, specular reflections, and pure local persistence.

- [x] Concentric radius law: R_inner = R_outer - Padding
- [x] Directional specular catchlights on top edges
- [ ] Export complete vault archive as portable Markdown
```

Every piece of document metadata—title, creation date, folder hierarchy, tags, and status flags—lives right at the top of the plain-text file in a convention understood across nearly every modern writing ecosystem.

## Bridging Browser Speed and File Longevity

There is a natural engineering trade-off in web applications: browsers do not let a static web tab read and write arbitrary folders on your desktop hard drive without repeated permission prompts. Inside the browser sandbox, `IndexedDB` (`fold_db`) provides fast, asynchronous structured storage so searching, filtering, and typing execute in under a millisecond.

To ensure that convenience never becomes a cage, Fold treats **two-way Markdown portability** as a first-class workflow rather than a hidden settings link:

| Capability | How Fold Implements Portability | Interoperability Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single Note Copy | Converts editor state directly to Markdown on clipboard | Paste cleanly into GitHub, Notion, Linear, or email |
| Individual `.md` Export | Downloads `<sanitized-title>.md` with full formatting | Any plain-text editor, Obsidian, or iA Writer |
| Foldered `.zip` Vault | Assembles folder subdirectories (`Inbox/`, `Ideas/`, `Work/`, `Journal/`) via client-side `JSZip` | Instant drop-in archive for desktop vaults & Git repos |
| Lossless `.json` Snapshot | Exports full `Note` schema plus embeds `fold-vault-manifest.json` in ZIP exports | 1-click full fidelity restoration back into Fold |
| Drag-and-Drop Import | Parses incoming `.md`, `.txt`, and `.json` files back into rich TipTap tasks & headings | Zero-friction migration into Fold |

## Building a "Long Now" Archive Habit

Technology changes faster than human memory. Adopting a file-first mindset requires only three lightweight habits:

- [x] Prefer tools that export human-readable `.md` files with open YAML metadata
- [x] Keep periodic `.zip` vault snapshots outside browser-managed cache (for example, an encrypted drive, local `Documents/` folder, or Time Machine backup)
- [x] Test restoring or opening an exported note in an unrelated plain-text editor once a quarter

When your notes can be opened with zero proprietary runtime—and imported or exported in seconds without asking permission from a server—you stop worrying about whether your favorite app will still exist ten years from now. The files belong to you, and the tool simply earns its place on your screen each day.
